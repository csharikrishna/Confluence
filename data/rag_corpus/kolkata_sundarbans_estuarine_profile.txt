KOLKATA & SUNDARBANS ESTUARINE ENVIRONMENTAL PROFILE
Document Reference: WB-PCB-SUNDAR-2023-ENV
Published Date: 2023-11-20
Authority: West Bengal Pollution Control Board & Sundarban Biosphere Reserve Directorate

1. DELTA HYDROLOGY & TIDAL AMPLITUDE
The lower Ganges-Brahmaputra delta and Hooghly estuary represent a dynamic, macrotidal estuarine ecosystem.
- Tidal Range: Semidiurnal tidal amplitude reaches 3.8m to 4.8m during spring tides at Sagar Island and Diamond Harbour. During neap tides, tidal range is 1.8m to 2.4m.
- Estuarine Wave Dynamics: Open sea swell in the outer Bay of Bengal averages 1.2m to 1.8m, but dissipates rapidly within the sheltered mangrove channels to 0.4m to 0.8m.
- Sea Surface Temperature & Salinity: Surface water temperature ranges from 22.0°C in January to 31.5°C in May. Salinity varies sharply with seasonal monsoon discharge (5 PSU to 28 PSU).

2. MONSOON & POST-MONSOON CLIMATOLOGY
- High Humidity Characteristics: Relative humidity along the coastal wetland belt remains consistently elevated, typically 78% to 94% year-round.
- Nor'wester (Kalbaishakhi) Events: Severe localized convective squalls occur primarily in April–May, generating wind gusts up to 65–85 km/h for 30–60 minutes.
- Tropical Cyclones: Historical landfalls in the Sundarbans (e.g., Aila, Amphan, Yaas) produce storm surges exceeding 4.0m, inundating low-lying earthen embankments.

3. AIR QUALITY & TRANSBOUNDARY PARTICULATE TRANSPORT
- Winter Particulate Spikes: Between November and February, shallow boundary layers trap urban and agricultural smoke, driving PM2.5 levels to 110–180 µg/m³ (Poor to Very Poor).
- Summer Monsoon Dispersion: During July–August, persistent southwesterly winds and heavy precipitation cleanse the air column, reducing PM2.5 to 15–28 µg/m³.
